Study design

Population

  • Adults with a bioprosthetic aortic valve (SAVR or TAVR), alive and failure-free at the reference echo, 30 to 90 days after implant.
  • Risk re-estimated at every visit: 6 months, 1 year, then yearly to 10 years.
  • Excluded: mechanical valves, a second prosthetic valve, non-structural dysfunction at baseline.

Endpoints, VARC-3

  • Stage 2 or worse deterioration against the patient's own reference echo, or reintervention for structural failure, within 5 years (also 2 and 8).
  • Death is a competing event, not censoring.

What is not deterioration

  • Endocarditis, thrombosis, paravalvular leak: censored, not counted. 8 of 18 candidate events in the extract.
  • Patient–prosthesis mismatch is a risk factor, not an event.

Definitions from VARC-3: Généreux P et al., Eur Heart J 2021;42:1825–1857. Reintervention counts only for a structural indication

"The endpoint is not ours, it is VARC-3, because an endpoint that does not match the literature compares with nothing. Patient-prosthesis mismatch is not one of the eight. The valve was small from day one, so it never deteriorated; it enters the model as a risk factor, not as an event. If asked about adjudication: the code proposes, two blinded cardiologists decide, a third settles disagreements, and kappa is reported. Say plainly that this is planned in the protocol and that nothing in the supplied extract has been adjudicated yet. If asked about secondary endpoints: valve failure alone, severe deterioration at stage 3, gradient progression and all-cause death, each reported separately and never pooled. If asked why patients with no reference echo are kept: dropping them would select the well-followed, so they are kept and analysed separately under a single-echo rule.
